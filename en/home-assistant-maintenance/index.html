MAINTENANCE

Home Assistant maintenance: how to keep your system stable for years

Author: Tomasz Furdal · HA Expert

Home Assistant can run reliably for a long time, but it is also a system that is developed very actively. New versions, integration changes, add-on updates and changes in device support appear regularly.

In a small installation, an update may often be just a few clicks. In a larger system, however, it is worth knowing what is changing and whether all parts of the installation still work together correctly.

Good maintenance does not mean constantly fixing Home Assistant. It means keeping the system in good condition, updating it carefully and noticing problems early before they start affecting everyday use.

UPDATES

Updates are a normal part of Home Assistant

Updates may affect not only Home Assistant itself, but also the operating system, add-ons, integrations and devices connected to the installation.

Not every update has to be installed immediately after release.

Before a larger change, it is worth checking the release information, known issues and which parts of the installation may be affected.

AFTER A CHANGE

What can change after an update?

A new version may affect:

  • integrations;
  • entities;
  • automations;
  • add-ons;
  • communication with devices;
  • functions that have been changed or removed.

This does not mean that every update causes problems. A larger installation simply has more dependencies, so after an important update it is worth checking that the most important functions still work as expected.

AVAILABILITY

Unavailable entities and devices that stop responding

One sign that requires attention is an entity becoming unavailable or a device no longer responding.

The cause is not always Home Assistant itself. It may be an integration, the network, Zigbee, the device, an external service or a change introduced by an update.

AUTOMATIONS

Automations after changes

More advanced automations often depend on several entities and integrations.

If one of them changes the way it reports its state or becomes unavailable, the automation may behave differently than before.

After larger changes, it is worth checking the most important scenarios, especially heating, alarm, presence, energy management and other functions that are important in everyday use.

EXTENSIONS

Integrations, add-ons and HACS

A system may use both official Home Assistant integrations and additional extensions.

If custom integrations or HACS are used, it is worth checking their current status and compatibility with the Home Assistant version before updating.

HACS is a convenient way to use community extensions. It simply adds another layer of dependencies to consider when planning a larger update.

DIAGNOSTICS

Logs, warnings and Repairs

Home Assistant can report problems before they become obvious in daily use.

Repeated errors, warnings and Repairs messages may indicate an integration that needs attention, a connection problem or a configuration that should be changed before a future update.

This does not mean reading all logs every day. The important part is reacting to meaningful and repeated messages that affect the installation.

BACKUP

Backup before a major change

A backup is a basic safeguard before a major update or configuration change.

Before making a larger change, it is worth checking that a current backup exists and that you know where it is stored.

Keeping a copy away from the device running Home Assistant is good practice. A backup is a natural part of proper system care, not a substitute for diagnosing a problem.

SYSTEM HEALTH

Disk space, database and overall system health

Not every problem comes from integrations.

It is also worth keeping an eye on:

  • available disk space;
  • data growing unexpectedly;
  • database problems;
  • repeated errors;
  • the state of basic system services.

A correctly configured database does not need constant manual administration.

TIMING

When should you update immediately and when is it better to wait a little?

There is no single rule for every installation.

Security updates and important fixes may justify a faster update.

With a larger installation, it can be sensible to first check the release information and confirm that the integrations you rely on are working correctly.

Staying on old versions for many months without a reason is not recommended.

PRIORITIES

The most important thing is the stability of the functions you actually use

Not every entity has the same importance.

In one home, heating may be the most important system. In another, it may be the alarm, blinds, energy management, ventilation or multimedia.

Good Home Assistant maintenance should focus first on the functions that the user actually depends on every day.

CHOICE

Do you have to maintain everything yourself?

No.

Some users enjoy following every change and maintaining the whole system themselves.

Others simply want to use their automations and know that if something goes wrong, there is someone who understands Home Assistant and can take care of the technical side.

Ongoing care can therefore make sense not only for someone who has no time, but also for someone who does not want to analyse updates, integration compatibility and problems after changes on their own.
